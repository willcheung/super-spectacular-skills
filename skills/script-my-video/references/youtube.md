# Long-form YouTube

A long video is a build, explanation or story told in chapters. Choose duration for the promised experience; 8–20 minutes is a starting scope, not a required range. Prefer a lasting question and firsthand evidence when the project's strategy calls for evergreen work. A timely event can introduce that question.

## Shape

1. **Cold open (0–30 s):** the most interesting moment or result first, before any intro. The title's promise starts paying off here.
2. **Intro (≤20 s):** who the creator is in one line, what the video will show, why it's worth the time. No channel trailer, no "before we start, subscribe".
3. **Chapters:** each with
   - a plain title (it becomes a YouTube chapter and an on-screen chapter card),
   - a bridge line, word for word, from the chapter before,
   - the question the creator answers to camera, with talking points and 1–2 written punchlines,
   - the demo or story on the real screen,
   - a takeaway line,
   - **a reason to keep watching:** an open question or a teased result the next chapter resolves. Chapter breaks are where long videos lose people.
4. **Recap:** only if the video is long enough that viewers need it. Decisions or tips, not a summary of what they just watched.
5. **End screen (last 20 s):** what to watch next, plus the follow line.

## The challenge format

A strong shape for a build video: **a person, a transformation, a time limit** ("a total beginner ships an app in 7 days"). Chapters follow the days or stages, each ending on the next open question ("we'll check the numbers tomorrow"). Plan a real low point before the win (the first result is bad, someone wants to quit) and don't skip it in the edit: the comeback only lands if the setback was shown. Never stage a setback that didn't happen.

## The personal-story format

Another strong shape, used only when the creator has a true story with real life stakes (a career turn, a product they shut down, a choice about their kids). It's one option, not a default; never bend a tutorial or tool video into it, and never invent feelings or events.

- **The stakes are the creator's life, tied to a fear the audience already has** (their job, AI, time with family). Viewers click to feel seen, not to learn a tool.
- **The title names a real event, consequence or feeling that matters to this viewer.** Use a number only when it is supported and useful. A feeling-led title is one option, not a universal Home rule.
- **The thumbnail adds something to the title.** Use a real expression or proof object where it serves the story; choose text length by actual feed-size legibility and the creator's preferences.
- **The first 15 seconds give the event and the named feelings.** No wind-up, no channel intro.
- **Concrete memories carry it:** one specific moment per chapter (a person, a place, a number), not abstractions.
- **Hold the best twist until near the end.** The title's open loop ("how did it happen?") carries the middle; the twist pays it off.
- **Almost no production:** one honest shot in a real room can be enough. Add receipts where they prove something, not to decorate.
- **End by sharing, not selling** ("I'm documenting this for anyone in the same spot"), then the CTA.

Sarcasm and sincerity can coexist: keep the creator's humor in the asides, and let the turning points land straight.

## Plan extras

- **Research notes:** what you read and what it changed, separate from the sources table.
- **Chapter list with rough timestamps,** written for the description.
- **A still of the biggest visual moment** (one or two), rendered before filming so the creator can see the look.
- **Title and thumbnail:** choose for Search, Home or Suggested and check the pair against the opening and payoff. Compare supportable packages with a stated hypothesis; changing both title and image tests a package. Use [the packaging method](../../video-director/references/packaging.md) for final upload preparation.
- **Clips of other people's talks or videos:** keep them short and comment on them (fair use). Never re-upload a talk.
- **Sources go in the description.**

## Filming

- **Record in segments the creator can redo separately,** one chapter at a time, each opening with its bridge line.
- **Screen-recorded demos:** script the talking points and the clicks, not every word. Record the real thing slowly, with a visible cursor.
- **Screen and face recorded separately** (silent screen capture, face-cam carries the audio): record the screen first, one file per chapter or demo, named by chapter (`03-lessons-file.mov`). Then film the face-cam while the recording plays on a monitor, so the creator describes what's actually on screen and the words line up with the clicks. Keep one camera position for the whole session, with room on one side for a picture-in-picture, and say cues out loud ("screen: the config part", "redo that").
- **Pickups:** after the first pass, list any missing lines or proof shots the cut needs and film them in one short session.

## Shorts from a long video

A build usually produces 1–3 Shorts as well. Lines pulled from the middle of a long video almost always start with a wind-up or depend on what came before, so:

- **Pick the Shorts while planning,** not after the edit: which chapters hold a standalone idea with its own receipt.
- **Give each Short its own hook variants,** word for word, filmed in the same session as standalone takes.
- **Give each its own close and CTA.** A chapter's takeaway line is rarely a good Short ending.
- Write each Short's clips with [shorts.md](shorts.md) rules, and mark which long-video footage it reuses.
