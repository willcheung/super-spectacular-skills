# Shorts (TikTok, Reels, YouTube Shorts)

One idea, one receipt, 35–50 seconds. Longer only when the story clearly earns it: full-watch rate drops sharply past a minute unless the stakes are universal.

## Shape

| Beat | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 s | The claim, number or verdict, as the first spoken words. First frame and cover text land with it. |
| Setup | 3–10 s | The one idea in one sentence, or the situation that makes the hook true. A short signpost into the story ("Here's what I mean."). |
| Proof | 10–35 s | What happened, shown on the real screen. One concrete moment beats three vague ones. When the story has a setback (it broke, it got it wrong, the first number was bad), put it here and then the comeback: it keeps the arc from being predictable. This is where most of the on-screen work goes. |
| Payoff | 35–45 s | What it means for the viewer: a decision rule, a lesson, or the twist. Must pay off the hook's promise. |
| Close | last 3–5 s | A callback or a question for the comments, then the CTA. No "this is the kind of stuff I…" warm-up. |

## Rules

- **One idea.** If you're explaining two things, it's two Shorts. Put the second in the brief's "not in this one" line and tease it in the close if it's next.
- **Proof early.** Show the real thing (screen, result, object) by about 5 s. Description without proof loses people.
- **Lines of 5–12 words.** Each line should survive being cut on its own; short lines give the edit more cut points.
- **Say numbers the way you'd say them out loud:** "three hours", "a whole month of tokens in one weekend".
- **Joke cadence follows the creator's voice.** For a high-energy sarcastic style, one written punchline every 10–15 seconds, each on its own line so the edit can land a meme beat on it.
- **The close earns the comment.** A specific question the viewer can answer in a few words ("What job would you give it?") beats "let me know what you think".
- **Cover text = the hook in 3–6 big words.** The caption repeats the promise and ends with a question.

## Several Shorts from one session

When one recording session feeds two or more Shorts, give each its own letter (A1–A4, B1–B6) and its own hook variants, bridge lines and close. Each must stand alone: someone who never saw Short A has to follow Short B. Teasing the next one in a close is fine; depending on it isn't.

## Explainer Shorts

When the Short explains a concept (what a token is, why an AI forgets), the explanation needs a picture. Give that beat one short animated explainer (3–8 s, one idea, then back to the face or a real receipt), and still back it with the creator's own real example. A generic explainer anyone could make is the thing to avoid.
