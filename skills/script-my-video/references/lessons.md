# Lessons from past scripts

General lessons that apply to anyone's script. Newest last. Format: date — rule — why. After each round of notes, add a line here if the lesson is general; if it's one creator's taste (their sign-off, humor level), put it in their voice profile instead. Write the why as a general reason, without per-video stats, titles, or view counts; those stay in the creator's own private tracker.

- 2026-10-10 — The first spoken words are the hook itself; no wind-up like "I actually tried…". — Most viewers decide in the first two seconds, and a hook that opens on the claim holds more of them than a wind-up, even against heavier graphics.
- 2026-10-10 — Lead with the human stakes; name the tool second. — Universal stakes need no context; a product or model name only lands with people who follow the news.
- 2026-10-11 — Write questions and talking points for the middle clips, not lines to read; only the hook and close are word for word. — Creators who riff sound natural answering a question and stiff reciting, and the edit only uses what they actually say.
- 2026-10-11 — Verify every number's origin before filming. — A striking stat nobody can trace back has to be re-measured or dropped, and finding that out after filming wastes the take.
- 2026-10-12 — Every clip opens with a word-for-word bridge line from the clip before, and the clips are filmed in order in one recording. — One-liners recorded separately across sessions cut together like random scenes: no line hands off to the next, and the edit can't invent the connection.
- 2026-10-12 — Plan one visual thread that runs the whole video and a transition out of every beat. — A new card on every beat resets the viewer every few seconds, even when the lines flow.
- 2026-10-13 — Write 1–2 punchlines per clip word for word. — Jokes depend on exact wording; a talking point like "make a joke about X" rarely lands when riffed.
- 2026-10-13 — Screen captures come before filming, and the script follows what they show. — A capture can contradict the plan (a status that reads "degraded", a timestamp later than claimed); finding that after filming means reshooting the line.
- 2026-10-18 — Run three idea tests before scripting: zero context, a setback and comeback, an open loop. — Most weak videos are weak ideas; no hook or edit fixes an idea a stranger can't follow or a story with no tension.
- 2026-10-18 — A hook opens a loop; it doesn't have to be a question. — Statements with an obvious "why?" hold viewers as well as questions do; what loses them is a hook that already gives away the result.
- 2026-10-18 — Write a separate title or caption per platform. — The same video can win in a swipe feed and flop in search, or the reverse; swipe feeds reward human stakes and YouTube rewards the words people search for.
- 2026-10-18 — When the creator has a specific receipt for the hook (a count, a dollar figure, a result), use it over a familiar frame like "you're doing X wrong". — Viewers have heard the generic frame many times and swipe before it lands; a specific number from the creator's own data is new to them.
- 2026-10-18 — Source ideas from the audience: a swipe file of the niche's top posts (borrow hook shapes, not content), unanswered questions in comments, and the viewer's own words for their problems. — Ideas pulled out of thin air guess at demand; the comments and top posts already show it.
- 2026-10-18 — Only make videos the creator has earned the right to explain. — Lived experience is what makes the video credible and uncopyable; a topic they'd need to research first reads like everyone else's.
- 2026-10-20 — For long-form, consider a true personal story as one option (not a default): the stakes are the creator's life and a fear the audience shares, the title names feelings plus a big true number, the thumbnail is one word over a real face, and the best twist is held until near the end. Don't bend tutorials or tool videos into it. — A sincere first-person story with universal stakes can travel on browse with almost no production, because viewers click to feel seen, not to learn a tool; forced emotion reads as fake.
- 2026-10-20 — A title for the home feed names a feeling the viewer already has, in the creator's true words; the search phrase moves to the description and chapter titles. — Identity lines ("I'm not a YouTuber") and how-to titles describe the creator or the task, so a browsing viewer has nothing to feel and no reason to click.
- 2026-10-20 — A long-form cold open is 20–30 seconds: the event or decision in the title's own words, how long the creator has been at it, the feeling or why it matters, and a one-line roadmap. Keep one-line punchy hooks for Shorts and mid-video re-hooks. — High-performing personal long-form videos open plainly on the event with a big true number of years; a Short-style zinger alone gives a browsing viewer no stakes for a 10-minute commitment.
