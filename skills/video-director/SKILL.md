# Video Director

Start at the requested stage. A packaging request needs a package, not a new interview or edit. Use the creator's existing plan and tools; load only the references needed for this task.

## Orient

Read the project's `AGENTS.md`/`CLAUDE.md` for the tracker, current findings, plans, profiles, research and production locations. Read the current plan and latest relevant feedback. Use existing context before asking for missing material. With no project setup, infer a useful brief from the request and ask only for gaps that change the work.

Confirm the format and discovery surface: Short versus long-form; Shorts feed, Home, Suggested or Search. Separate supplied facts, creator preferences, observed results and hypotheses. Keep private account results in their existing tracker.

## Choose a topic

1. Start from the current queue, viewer questions and real material the creator can show. Reuse the existing lanes and production statuses.
2. For each serious candidate, complete the viewer sentence from the scripting brief: what someone wants from watching, and what this video can actually deliver. Its seven viewer motivations are planning options, not audience segments measured by a dashboard.
3. Identify the firsthand event, build, result, artifact or decision that makes the video this creator's. Gather missing evidence before treating an appealing claim as film-ready.
4. Compare a few relevant creators when outside research would change the choice. Read titles, thumbnails, actual openings and viewer questions. When comparing public performance, prefer comparable formats and upload ages; a multiple over a channel's recent median can identify an outlier, but does not explain its cause. State the comparison set and sample limits. Google search volume is not YouTube search volume.
5. Recommend one next video and explain its tradeoff. Rank more only when helpful. Name preparation effort and the largest unresolved gap; avoid invented demand, CTR, revenue or virality estimates. Preserve the creator's chosen thesis and queue unless asked to change them.

## Long-form check

Read the actual outline, transcript or edit and its promised viewer payoff. Ask whether the opening starts delivering the title/thumbnail promise, each chapter advances a real question or consequence, proof is legible, and the ending earns the promised answer or perspective. Locate likely drop-off risks in the material rather than imposing a timer for interruptions or jokes. Quote actual lines when proposing cuts; new spoken lines require a pickup and the creator's voice.

Review long-form on its own terms. Shorts 2-second/5-second predictions, swipe scores and fixed visual-change cadences do not apply. A mixed long-form/Shorts shoot sheet needs separate checks. If an independent second opinion is requested, give a fresh reviewer the actual material and applicable creator constraints without the editor's defense; retain the format distinction.

## Preserve what was learned

Current instructions should resolve historical conflicts explicitly. Read dated lessons for their reasoning, scope and exceptions; preserve the entries when a rule is qualified or superseded. A single release creates a hypothesis, not a universal performance rule. Creator preferences remain preferences even when there is no performance measurement.

| Information | Existing owner |
| --- | --- |
| Audience, lanes, queue, private metrics and current decisions | Project tracker and its Current findings / Results log |
| Sources, alternative explanations and dated research | Project research library |
| General story, filming and hook lessons | Scripting references and dated lessons |
| General cutting, sound, visuals and delivery lessons | Editing references and dated lessons |
| Writing taste / visual and audio taste | Voice profile / brand profile |
| Review prediction errors | Reviewer's calibration notes; actual numbers stay private |

Update the owner rather than create another playbook. Link supporting research instead of copying its metrics into public skills. Preparation does not authorize publishing, messaging, scheduling, paid services or account changes. Follow the project's preview and render approvals.
