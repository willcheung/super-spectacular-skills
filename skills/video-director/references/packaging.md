# Packaging the actual video

Read the current plan, viewer brief, final cut or transcript, creator voice/brand profile, and prior package before making options. Keep approved facts and creative choices. At planning time use provisional chapter times; an upload-ready package requires timings from the final cut.

## Title, thumbnail and opening together

Name the intended discovery surface. Search packaging should make the requested task or question clear; Home and Suggested packaging should make the story and viewer stake clear. A tool name belongs where it helps the viewer recognize the promise. Neither an emotion-only title nor a keyword-first title is universal.

Prepare a small number of distinct, supportable concepts, usually two or three. For each, record:

| Title | Thumbnail text / real visual | Viewer question or promise | What the thumbnail adds | Proof in the opening | What this variant tests |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Recommend one with a concrete tradeoff. Check the pair for duplicated meaning, vague claims, misleading implications, likely truncation and readability at feed size. Shared topic words can help orientation; assess whether the image/text adds information rather than enforcing zero shared words. Use a real frame or proof object where available and honor the creator's expression and design preferences. Thumbnail word count follows legibility, not a universal one-word or three-word ceiling.

For tests, state the hypothesis and changed variable. Thumbnail-only changes can test a visual idea; changing title and thumbnail together compares packages. Use the available native test and its reported outcome, with no fabricated winner or predicted uplift. Sequential swaps and unrelated uploads do not isolate causality. Preserve previous packages and record changes in the project's existing notes.

## Delivery package

Include what this format and destination need:

- Selected title and matched thumbnail; alternatives only when useful.
- Description opening that truthfully continues the promise, followed by useful resources, source links and relevant disclosures. Retain necessary context rather than hiding facts to manufacture curiosity.
- Final chapter timestamps and plain labels for long-form. Check current platform requirements when preparing a live upload; verify every boundary against the final cut. Never present invented or provisional times as final.
- Native captions or corrected `.srt` for long-form where requested; check proper nouns and factual terms against the audio. Burned-in captions follow format and creator preference.
- A relevant next video for end screen, playlist or Shorts Related video where available. Recommend based on the next viewer question, not a generic subscription request.
- A specific viewer question for the description or pinned comment when useful. Drafting a comment does not authorize posting it.

Keep tags and hashtags proportional to their role; do not turn packaging into a tag exercise. Verify platform specifications as needed instead of maintaining speculative benchmark tables.

## Reuse without retelling everything

For a Short from long-form, choose a self-contained result, story or practical moment with its own payoff, hook and real proof. Check for a story already published. Write a new hook for a pickup through the scripting skill, or select an existing spoken line for an edit; never substitute newly written speech for footage.

For a blog or social excerpt, reuse the source facts and adapt the shape to that audience through the writing skill. Prepare only the channels the creator actually uses or requested. A publishing cadence, new channel or scheduled job needs its own decision.
