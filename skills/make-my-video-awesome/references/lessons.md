# Lessons from past edits

General lessons that apply to anyone's video. Newest last. Format: date — rule — why. After each round of notes, add a line here if the lesson is general; if it's one creator's taste (a handle, music, a color preference), put it in that creator's profile instead (the owner's is brand-profile.md). Write the why as a general reason, without per-video stats, titles, or view counts; those stay in the creator's own private tracker.

- 2026-10-08 — End on the CTA itself; cut lead-ins before it ("This is the kind of stuff I…"). — Warm-ups before the CTA lose viewers at the very end.
- 2026-10-08 — Remove filler fragments at seams (a lone "during", "and, but") even when the transcript hides them; listen with energy.py. — They read as mistakes.
- 2026-10-08 — When the creator says they'll add their own overlay for a moment, leave that slot clear instead of filling it with invented reactions. — Real material beats illustrative filler.
- 2026-10-08 — Sarcastic lines get a meme beat (top/bottom meme text, vignette, punch-in, a flipping upside-down smiley). Use the 🙃-style face, not "/s". — "/s" isn't widely understood.
- 2026-10-08 — When the speaker talks about their own data (usage, revenue, analytics), put their real screenshot full-screen and annotate it in sync (baseline, highlight, measured multiplier). Covering the face is fine. — Proof beats description.
- 2026-10-09 — Color: match the HyperFrames Studio preview. Never brighten whites in a grade. — Earlier renders were judged "washed out / white"; the Studio look was the reference.
- 2026-10-09 — Pieces shorter than ~0.25 s flash on screen; cut.py merges them. — Two- and five-frame shots read as glitches.
- 2026-10-09 — Real screenshots and screen recordings over recreated or AI-generated UI whenever they exist; generate only when nothing real is available. Point out moments where real material would help. — Short-form audiences favor genuine content; recreated chat cards read as AI-made.
- 2026-10-09 — Before/after must be honest about timing: the "after" shown at a failure beat must be the failed output, not the eventual fix. — Showing the fixed result at "terrible" tells the opposite story.
- 2026-10-09 — Numbered lessons build up in one card (1 → 1,2 → 1,2,3) instead of a separate card per lesson. — The growing list reads as a checklist and ends on a complete, save-worthy frame.
- 2026-10-09 — Top cards start at y ≈ 165 (was ≈ 120). — Instagram's feed username/audio row covers about y 90–195; at 120 the chip was hidden and at 140 it still touched. ≈ 250 was tried and blocks the face.
- 2026-10-10 — The first spoken words must be the hook itself; cut wind-ups like "I actually tried…" so the claim lands in the first second. — Most viewers decide in the first two seconds; a hook that opens on the claim holds more of them than a wind-up, even against heavier graphics.
- 2026-10-10 — Aim for 35–50s on Shorts unless the story clearly earns more. — Full-watch rate drops sharply on longer cuts; only universal stakes keep people past a minute.
- 2026-10-12 — Transcribe with Parakeet (`npx hyperframes models install parakeet`), not Whisper. — Whisper stretched words across long pauses by seconds, so cut points landed in the wrong place; Parakeet's word times were tight enough to cut from directly.
- 2026-10-12 — Pay off the hook with something concrete: if the hook is a number ("22 jobs"), show what those jobs are (the creator's own receipt), not just that they exist. — An independent review flagged that an unanswered hook promise is where viewers swipe.
- 2026-10-12 — Receipts must be readable at phone size: a still zoomed to the one line beats a fast scroll, and dark text-heavy screens under captions read as noise. Keep the face on screen through the first 5 s; put the receipt in a strip under the hook card instead of a full takeover. — The 2→5 s window decides the 5 s hold.
- 2026-10-12 — Cap back-to-back full-screen takeovers at ~8–10 s; between them, bring the face back or use a card/strip. — Long faceless stretches with no music lose the full-watch viewers.
- 2026-10-12 — End on the comment question; drop a generic "follow for more" sign-off. — Viewers swipe as soon as a sign-off starts, and ending on a question loops cleanly.
- 2026-10-12 — When a receipt's numbers look different from the spoken claim (promo vs renewal price), box the line that matches what was said instead of showing the whole page. — Skeptical commenters catch mismatches.
- 2026-10-12 — Cohesion beats coverage. Script each clip to open with a bridge line that answers the previous one ("So…", "But here's the catch…"), film the clips in order in one continuous recording, and in the dress carry one visual object across beats (it evolves, moves, transforms) instead of dropping a new card per beat. — A Short assembled from standalone one-liners, each with its own card, reads as random scenes strung together, no matter how well each piece is cut.
- 2026-10-14 — News receipts are a full-screen excerpt of the real article (headline, date, and the paragraph or list with the claim), with the backing phrases highlighted as spoken; not a one-line zoom. Real screenshots that carry a beat (an app screen, a pricing page) go big over the face too. — Small top-band cards and tight zooms read as decoration; a whole, legible excerpt proves the source is real.
- 2026-10-14 — Never cut a sentence before its payoff. If the speaker says "X is so powerful because…", keep the reason clause; trim filler inside it instead ("the hidden news is that"). — A claim with its "because" cut off sounds unfinished and makes the next beat feel random.
- 2026-10-14 — When a jump cut joins two moments far apart in a take (light, position or energy changes), carry an overlay across the cut: flip or morph the card on the cut frame instead of removing it there, and give the next card content from its first frame (placeholders that fill in on the words). — A card leaving exactly on a mismatched cut exposes the jump and reads as a rough transition.
- 2026-10-15 — When a speaker runs a word straight into the next one, the transcript can call the first word finished while the cut still carries a held breath and the onset of the next word ("for you…in-"). Find the real end by transcribing the clip at a few candidate end points and taking the earliest that still keeps the whole word, then add a short (~90 ms) fade-out on that seam. — Energy dips alone don't show a soft word onset, and viewers hear the fragment even when the transcript doesn't.
- 2026-10-18 — When delivering the render, hand over packaging per platform: cover text and caption for TikTok/Reels (human stakes), a search-led title for YouTube Shorts. — The same cut performs very differently by platform, and the words around it are most of that difference.
- 2026-10-18 — Never trim a real setback out to make the story cleaner. — The comeback is what holds viewers to the end, and it only lands if they saw the thing go wrong.
- 2026-10-18 — Footage recorded on the phone mic in an echoey room: set `"voice": "clean"` in edit.json. — It cuts rumble, hiss and the echo tail between words with plain ffmpeg; it won't sound like a studio, but it reads as clear and still authentic. Suggest a clip-on mic for next time.
- 2026-10-18 — Keep it platform-native and lightly produced: phone-shot, real places, raw narration; heavy polish, studio looks and stock filler usually lower views. — Viewers trust what looks like it belongs in their feed; edits should add clarity (cuts, captions, zooms on proof), not production value.
- 2026-10-18 — Cut any end-of-video summary. — Retention drops at the recap; viewers already got it.
