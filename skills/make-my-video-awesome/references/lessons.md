# Lessons from past edits

General lessons that apply to anyone's video. Newest last. Format: date — rule — why. After each round of notes, add a line here if the lesson is general; if it's one creator's taste (a handle, music, a color preference), put it in that creator's profile instead (the owner's is brand-profile.md). Write the why as a general reason, without per-video stats, titles, or view counts; those stay in the creator's own private tracker.

- 2026-10-08 — End on the CTA itself; cut lead-ins before it ("This is the kind of stuff I…"). — Warm-ups before the CTA lose viewers at the very end.
- 2026-10-08 — Remove filler fragments at seams (a lone "during", "and, but") even when the transcript hides them; listen with energy.py. — They read as mistakes.
- 2026-10-08 — When the creator says they'll add their own overlay for a moment, leave that slot clear instead of filling it with invented reactions. — Real material beats illustrative filler.
- 2026-10-08 — Sarcastic lines get a meme beat (top/bottom meme text, vignette, punch-in, a flipping upside-down smiley). Use the 🙃-style face, not "/s". — "/s" isn't widely understood.
- 2026-10-08 — When the speaker talks about their own data (usage, revenue, analytics), put their real screenshot full-screen and annotate it in sync (baseline, highlight, measured multiplier). Covering the face is fine. — Proof beats description.
- 2026-10-09 — Color: match the HyperFrames Studio preview. Never brighten whites in a grade. — Earlier renders were judged "washed out / white"; the Studio look was the reference.
- 2026-10-09 — Pieces shorter than ~0.25 s flash on screen; cut.py merges them. — Two- and five-frame shots read as glitches.
- 2026-10-09 — Real screenshots and screen recordings over recreated or AI-generated UI whenever they exist; generate only when nothing real is available. Point out moments where real material would help. — Short-form audiences favor genuine content; recreated chat cards read as AI-made.
- 2026-10-09 — Before/after must be honest about timing: the "after" shown at a failure beat must be the failed output, not the eventual fix. — Showing the fixed result at "terrible" tells the opposite story.
- 2026-10-09 — Numbered lessons build up in one card (1 → 1,2 → 1,2,3) instead of a separate card per lesson. — The growing list reads as a checklist and ends on a complete, save-worthy frame.
- 2026-10-09 — Top cards start at y ≈ 165 (was ≈ 120). — Instagram's feed username/audio row covers about y 90–195; at 120 the chip was hidden and at 140 it still touched. ≈ 250 was tried and blocks the face.
- 2026-10-10 — The first spoken words must be the hook itself; cut wind-ups like "I actually tried…" so the claim lands in the first second. — Most viewers decide in the first two seconds; a hook that opens on the claim holds more of them than a wind-up, even against heavier graphics.
- 2026-10-10 — Aim for 35–50s on Shorts unless the story clearly earns more. — Full-watch rate drops sharply on longer cuts; only universal stakes keep people past a minute.
- 2026-10-12 — Transcribe with Parakeet (`npx hyperframes models install parakeet`), not Whisper. — Whisper stretched words across long pauses by seconds, so cut points landed in the wrong place; Parakeet's word times were tight enough to cut from directly.
- 2026-10-12 — Pay off the hook with something concrete: if the hook is a number ("22 jobs"), show what those jobs are (the creator's own receipt), not just that they exist. — An independent review flagged that an unanswered hook promise is where viewers swipe.
- 2026-10-12 — Receipts must be readable at phone size: a still zoomed to the one line beats a fast scroll, and dark text-heavy screens under captions read as noise. Keep the face on screen through the first 5 s; put the receipt in a strip under the hook card instead of a full takeover. — The 2→5 s window decides the 5 s hold.
- 2026-10-12 — Cap back-to-back full-screen takeovers at ~8–10 s; between them, bring the face back or use a card/strip. — Long faceless stretches with no music lose the full-watch viewers.
- 2026-10-12 — End on the comment question; drop a generic "follow for more" sign-off. — Viewers swipe as soon as a sign-off starts, and ending on a question loops cleanly.
- 2026-10-12 — When a receipt's numbers look different from the spoken claim (promo vs renewal price), box the line that matches what was said instead of showing the whole page. — Skeptical commenters catch mismatches.
- 2026-10-12 — Cohesion beats coverage. Script each clip to open with a bridge line that answers the previous one ("So…", "But here's the catch…"), film the clips in order in one continuous recording, and in the dress carry one visual object across beats (it evolves, moves, transforms) instead of dropping a new card per beat. — A Short assembled from standalone one-liners, each with its own card, reads as random scenes strung together, no matter how well each piece is cut.
- 2026-10-14 — News receipts are a full-screen excerpt of the real article (headline, date, and the paragraph or list with the claim), with the backing phrases highlighted as spoken; not a one-line zoom. Real screenshots that carry a beat (an app screen, a pricing page) go big over the face too. — Small top-band cards and tight zooms read as decoration; a whole, legible excerpt proves the source is real.
- 2026-10-14 — Never cut a sentence before its payoff. If the speaker says "X is so powerful because…", keep the reason clause; trim filler inside it instead ("the hidden news is that"). — A claim with its "because" cut off sounds unfinished and makes the next beat feel random.
- 2026-10-14 — When a jump cut joins two moments far apart in a take (light, position or energy changes), carry an overlay across the cut: flip or morph the card on the cut frame instead of removing it there, and give the next card content from its first frame (placeholders that fill in on the words). — A card leaving exactly on a mismatched cut exposes the jump and reads as a rough transition.
- 2026-10-15 — When a speaker runs a word straight into the next one, the transcript can call the first word finished while the cut still carries a held breath and the onset of the next word ("for you…in-"). Find the real end by transcribing the clip at a few candidate end points and taking the earliest that still keeps the whole word, then add a short (~90 ms) fade-out on that seam. — Energy dips alone don't show a soft word onset, and viewers hear the fragment even when the transcript doesn't.
- 2026-10-18 — When delivering the render, hand over packaging per platform: cover text and caption for TikTok/Reels (human stakes), a search-led title for YouTube Shorts. — The same cut performs very differently by platform, and the words around it are most of that difference.
- 2026-10-18 — Never trim a real setback out to make the story cleaner. — The comeback is what holds viewers to the end, and it only lands if they saw the thing go wrong.
- 2026-10-25 — Default to `"voice": "none"` (raw audio, levelled only). Offer `"clean"` only for a clip with obvious rumble, hiss or echo, and have the creator listen before it goes on a whole video. — Its noise reduction and gate made a long-form voice sound processed and choppy; a phone mic in a quiet room sounds better untouched. (Replaces a 2026-10-18 note that recommended "clean" for echoey phone audio.)
- 2026-10-18 — Keep it platform-native and lightly produced: phone-shot, real places, raw narration; heavy polish, studio looks and stock filler usually lower views. — Viewers trust what looks like it belongs in their feed; edits should add clarity (cuts, captions, zooms on proof), not production value.
- 2026-10-18 — Cut any end-of-video summary. — Retention drops at the recap; viewers already got it.
- 2026-10-19 — Search raw transcripts for spoken edit cues ("cut that out", "do that again", "that one's good") before choosing takes. — Creators record long single takes and direct the edit out loud; ignoring the cue keeps a take they already rejected.
- 2026-10-21 — Show a one-line-per-beat plan (time, layout, what's on screen, words) and get a go before building the dress. — Beat-level changes (a reordered beat, a dropped card, a different receipt) cost a line of text before the build and a rebuild after it.
- 2026-10-24 — In long-form, don't fill the space beside the speaker with kinetic text and small cards on every line. Use full-screen takeovers for proof and concept explainers, return to the face between them, and keep at most one or two persistent elements on the set. — Side-panel animations compete with the face and read as clutter over minutes of runtime; a full-screen beat earns attention because the default frame is calm.
- 2026-10-24 — On a light set or over light screenshots, put captions on a dark rounded pill and show the whole phrase at once. — Cream text with a dark stroke reads over a dark shirt but turns into hollow outlines over light backgrounds, and word-by-word reveals inside a fixed pill show an empty bar.
- 2026-10-24 — For long-form (10+ min, many segments), cut and transcribe in chapter-sized chunks in parallel, then join with offsets. — One ffmpeg pass with ~100 trims of a 20-minute HEVC source crawled, and Parakeet garbled words after minute 2 of a 10-minute file; per-chunk runs were fast and clean.
- 2026-10-24 — After `remove-background`, check lip sync late in the clip: compare matte frames to the source's at the same timestamp. Its output ran 0.25–0.45% slow (0.4 s by minute 1.5), linear from zero; play each matte at a measured constant `data-playback-rate`. Trim later edits by placing matte ranges with `data-media-start`, not by re-matting. — Duration ratios were off by up to 0.1%; frame matching is exact, and re-matting costs minutes per chunk.
- 2026-10-25 — For long-form, set `"pauses": [0.65, 0.40]` in edit.json (cut.py keeps pauses up to 0.65 s and shortens longer ones to 0.4 s). — The Shorts default (0.30 → 0.14) made ~290 micro-cuts in a 9-minute video: viewers heard it as jittery audio and clipped words.
- 2026-10-25 — Explain a jargon word on screen the first time it's spoken in a video for a general audience (a lower-third: "IDE = the app programmers write code in"). — A long-form viewer who doesn't know the term stops following the point, and there's no caption to rescue them.
- 2026-10-25 — When full-screen takeovers run back to back (a headline montage), cut straight from one to the next; wipe in only the first one and never let the face show between them. — A face flashing between headlines reads as a glitch and breaks the run of tension.
- 2026-10-25 — Bring a persistent motif in when the story introduces it, and let it build as each piece is told (one brick per era), instead of showing it from the first frame. — On screen before it means anything, it's clutter, and it spoils its own reveal.
- 2026-10-25 — Don't leave a silent gap for a chapter card; lay the card over the chapter's first words. — A second of dead air in a talking-head video sounds like a broken edit.
- 2026-10-25 — Hook and cover text: name the viewer's stake ("we", "you"), not only the creator's. — A first-person line ("Am I next?") reads as one person's problem; an inclusive one ("Are we next?") puts the viewer in the video.
- 2026-10-25 — After a re-cut or new footage, audit every word anchor for a match that now lands earlier in the video (common words like "two weeks", "school", "founder"); scope anchors to their section (`after=`). — New footage reuses everyday words, and an overlay silently fires a minute early on the first match.
- 2026-10-25 — Don't show the same receipt twice in one video unless it's a deliberate callback. — Viewers read a repeated headline as filler and assume the evidence is thin.
- 2026-10-25 — Replace an illustrative mock-up (a fake chat bubble) with the creator's real screenshot rather than deleting its "illustration" label. — Unlabelled mock-ups pass as real; a real screen is honest and stronger.
- 2026-10-25 — When a reshoot was filmed in dimmer light, match it with `clip_grade` in edit.json: a midtone curves lift on those clips only, measured on the face (not the wall) against the older footage, checking nothing clips to white. — A bright background hides the difference in average brightness; the viewer sees the face get darker at the cut.
- 2026-10-26 — An overlay meant to sit on a full-screen screen recording (a sticker, a caption) must come after that video in the composition's HTML; a higher z-index alone didn't lift it above a video placed later. Check it in a snapshot. — The sticker rendered but was invisible under the video.
- 2026-10-26 — After assembling, scan for full-screen beats that end a hair (< ~0.35 s) before the next one starts and stretch the first to meet the second; when a screen recording follows another full-screen beat, show it instantly rather than sliding it in. — A 0.1 s gap flashes the speaker's face between two takeovers, which reads as a glitch; anchors on consecutive words leave these gaps everywhere.
- 2026-10-26 — A board must never sit near-empty waiting for its first item: give it something from the first frame (dashed slots its items drop into, the first item, a pull quote). Open it later only if there's camera footage to fall back on; over a VO slot there's no face behind it, just a blank frame. Scan verify.py's long still spans in the final render for this before handing over. — A board sat ~10 s with only its label in a final render; moving its start exposed a blank VO gap.
- 2026-10-26 — Covers: start from a real frame where the creator's face already shows the cover's emotion; sample densely around the emotional lines and seek accurately (`-ss` after `-i`). — A fast seek grabbed a different frame than the contact sheet showed, and AI-edited expressions of a real face were rejected as not looking like him.
- 2026-10-26 — Build covers as HTML in the video's own font and card style, render 1280×720 with headless Chrome, and check them at 320 px wide (feed size): text off the face and clear of the duration badge in the bottom-right. — HTML keeps the cover on-brand and makes a wording change a one-line edit; a cover that reads at full size can still fail in the feed.
- 2026-10-26 — A/B covers should test two ideas (a question in words versus a real proof object), not two wordings, and each cover should add to its paired title rather than repeat it. — YouTube's test picks by watch-time share and often calls no winner when the variants look alike; breakout thumbnails rarely repeat their titles.
- 2026-10-26 — Never give away the answer to the cover's question in the description. — The description shows in search and on desktop before the click; a take that answers the hook closes the loop that makes people watch.
- 2026-10-28 — Export long-form for YouTube at 4K (upscale the 1080p render with lanczos if the footage is 1080p), not 1080p. — On a small channel, YouTube gave 1080p uploads only a partial set of quality versions (missing rungs, VP9 only at 360p) and never filled it in; Auto then sat at 240p on desktop and 360p on iPhone. A 4K upload got the full set from 144p to 2160p in every codec.
