# Blog posts (Substack-style build logs and takes)

Use for articles and newsletters by anyone. This file covers the shape of a post; voice comes from the writer's own material (or, for Will, writer-voice-profile.md). For a substantial new piece in Will's voice, run the interview round in interview-led-writing.md first.

## What a good post is

A builder reporting a real experiment or decision, with enough specifics that a reader could repeat it or decide to skip it. The reader should finish knowing what was tried, what happened, what it cost (whatever the source gives: time, money, tokens; never estimate a cost the writer didn't give), and what the writer would do in their position.

## Default shape (flex it to the material)

1. **Title** that reports the result or the stance. Good: a first-person outcome plus a twist ("I Gave X a 3-Hour Job. You Probably Don't Need It."). Bad: a vague benefit ("Unlocking the Power of Long-Running Agents").
2. **Subtitle** (Substack): one plain sentence that adds the second half of the hook.
3. **Opening** (2–4 short paragraphs): what's happening or what the writer was trying to do, and why they cared. A disclaimer line if relevant (not sponsored, paid tier, not a tutorial).
4. **Context**: the project, briefly, for someone who hasn't read earlier posts. Link back to the earlier post instead of re-explaining.
5. **What I did**: goals, the actual prompt/spec, settings. Lists are fine. Show the prompt when it's interesting.
6. **What happened**: the honest result, with numbers and screenshots. Name the failure plainly in a heading if that's the story.
7. **What it means / my take**: the judgment, including when not to use the thing.
8. **Pro tips**: numbered, concrete, earned from this experiment. As many as the source supports, which may be one or none; don't pad.
9. **It's live / for the nerds**: links to the thing and the repo, if they exist.
10. **Sign-off**: optionally one genuine question asking readers for their input, or a subscribe/follow line. Not a recap, not a rhetorical question.

Length: 800–1,500 words suits most build logs (it's Will's usual range). Go longer only when the build genuinely has more steps.

## Formatting

- Plain-label H2s, sentence case or title case consistently within a post.
- Short paragraphs (1–3 sentences).
- Bold for the one number that matters (total time, total cost).
- Image placeholders where proof belongs: `[Screenshot: dashboard before/after]`, `[Embed: the post I'm reacting to]`. Don't describe images that don't exist as if they do.
- Substack inserts its own "Thanks for reading… subscribe" blocks; don't write them into the draft unless asked.

## Output

Return the title, subtitle, and post body ready to paste, then the short notes section the main skill asks for (What I developed / What changed / Assumptions), including any placeholder that needs a real screenshot or link.
